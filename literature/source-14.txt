SWELL authored source summary, not a quote. DOI 10.1029/2023GL107549. GPS at the University of Houston Coastal Center near Galveston, Texas, on Beaumont expansive clay. Summer droughts lowered the ground by about 6 to 16 centimetres in 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2023 as the clay dried and shrank, then most of it recovered with rain. Roughly a tenth of the drop did not recover (inelastic, permanent). The movement is confined to about the upper 4 metres above a stable water table. Built-up ground with pavement or foundations moved much less than open fields. Relevance to SWELL: supports clay shrink-swell driven by drying and wetting, the paving shield term, and an active zone of a few metres. Limitation for SWELL: the SWELL clay equation is fully recoverable and has no permanent inelastic term; GPS not radar, one site.
